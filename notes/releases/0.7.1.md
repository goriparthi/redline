A fresh install now speaks up on its own: threshold notifications and the cadence cues are both on out of the box.

### Changed: notifications and cadence cues are on by default

**Notify at Thresholds** and **Say How the Day Is Going** both shipped switched off, on the
reasoning that an interruption should be asked for. In practice that made a monitor which sat
silent until someone went looking for it, which is the opposite of what a menu bar monitor is
for. The whole point of watching a limit is being told before it arrives rather than after.

Neither one prompts macOS at launch. Permission is still asked for at the first delivery, when
there is finally something to say, because an app that asks to notify before it has anything
to report is an app people refuse once and forever. Nothing is posted until then either way.

The cues remain descriptive and silent: they say what was counted from your own timestamps and
stop, they never make a sound, and they can only fire when there has been recent activity, so
a machine nobody is using stays quiet. Limit alerts still make a sound only when a cap is
actually reached.

This changes the default, not your setting. An existing `~/.config/redline/config.json`
already carries an explicit value for both keys, so upgrading will not switch anything on or
off behind you. Both live under **Settings**, and as `alerts` and `mindfulCues` in the config.

### Install

    brew install --cask ./Casks/redline.rb

Or download the DMG below. Requires macOS Sonoma or later.
